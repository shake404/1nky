# Your tag

Your tag is your name here, and it's the only thing that identifies you. There is
no account behind it, no email attached to it, nothing on our side to look up. It
gets made on your device and it stays there.

This page is everything you can do with it. The plumbing underneath is on
[How it works](/how-it-works).

## Picking one

Land on the site → **PICK A TAG** → type a name → you're in. No email, no phone,
no CAPTCHA, no confirmation link, no terms wall.

There's a short **"spraying…"** wait the first time. That's your device doing a
little work to prove you're not a thousand bots — it costs you a couple of seconds
and it costs a spam farm real money. It happens again, much faster, every time you
put something up. You never have to do anything but wait for it.

::: tip Tags are not unique
There's no registrar, because a registrar would be the account system we refuse to
build. Two writers can pick the same name. That's what the **mark** is for.
:::

## Your mark

Next to every tag name is a six-character **mark** and a small generated glyph.
It's a fingerprint of your tag that can't be copied or claimed by anyone else.

- Same name **and** same mark → same writer.
- Same name, **different mark** → different writer. Somebody's biting.

Read the mark, not the name. Writer chips also carry **age dots** — how long the
tag has been around — so a name that appeared this morning doesn't look like one
that's been up for a year.

## Your face, your bio

**Setup → Edit your tag.** You get a bio, a city, the crews you're
[reppin'](/guide/crews#repping-vs-the-roster), and an avatar.

Avatars go through a **cropper** — drag and zoom until the picture is framed how
you want it — and then through the exact same scrub as a flick: the location and
camera details are destroyed on your device before the picture leaves it.

## Your blackbook

Your tag lives on your device, which means the device is the single point of
failure. Phones get lost. Browsers throw out storage. iPhones are especially rude
about it — Safari discards a site's storage after about a week of not opening it,
unless the site is [installed to your home screen](/guide/install).

Your **blackbook** is the fix. **Setup → Save your blackbook** locks your tag with
a passphrase you choose and hands you a file, plus a scannable block you can
photograph or print.

> Lose your blackbook, lose your tag. Nobody can recover it. Not even us.
> Especially not us.

That isn't a support dodge. There is no reset link because there is nothing on our
side to reset from — we never had your tag. It's the same property that makes this
place safe for you, and it cuts both ways.

**Do it right after your first post.** The app will nag you until you do. Let it.

::: warning Your blackbook is an opsec object
It's the one thing that can prove you're you — which means it's the one thing that
can prove you're you. Don't leave it unlocked in Downloads, don't put it in a
cloud drive under your legal name, don't screenshot it to a phone that auto-syncs
photos. A printed block in a physical place is a genuinely good backup.
[More on the opsec page.](/privacy/opsec)
:::

## Getting your tag back

**Already got a tag?** is the second door on the landing screen.

| You have | What to do |
|---|---|
| Your blackbook file | Restore → **Upload your blackbook** → enter the passphrase |
| A photo or printout of the block | Read it with your phone's camera, then Restore → **Or paste it here** → passphrase |
| A second device already set up | See "two devices" below |
| Neither, but recovery was switched on | See "recovery" below |
| Neither, and recovery was off | The tag is gone. Pick a new one. |

Restoring brings back the same name, the same mark, the same history.

## Two devices, one tag

**Setup → Link a device.** Enter a passphrase, and the app draws a scannable
block. Open 1NKY on the other device, choose **Already got a tag?**, read the block
with that device's camera, paste what it gives you, and type the passphrase. Same tag,
two devices.

::: danger That block is your tag
Anyone who photographs the block and knows the passphrase becomes you. Show it,
scan it, hide it.
:::

## Recovery (optional, off by default)

**Setup → Recovery** is a spare copy of your blackbook, locked with a passphrase,
left on our box.

- It is **off** unless you switch it on.
- We hold a locked file and nothing else. The passphrase never leaves your device,
  so we can't open it — not for you, not for anyone who asks us to.
- You get a recovery link. Save it; it's how you point at the locked copy later.
- Lose both the passphrase and the file and the tag is gone, same as always.

It exists because "lost the phone" is the most common way people lose a name, and
a locked file we can't read is a safer answer than no answer.

## Hanging it up

Writers retire names. **Setup → Hang it up** is that, done properly.

You type your tag name to arm it. Then the app goes through everything it can find
under your tag — what this device remembers and what's up on the wall — and buffs
it, tallying as it goes. It puts up one last note saying the tag is hung up, and
then the device forgets the tag entirely. If it gets interrupted, it picks up where
it left off.

It's a ritual, not an error state. What it can't do is un-ring a bell: anything
already screenshotted, downloaded or mirrored by somebody else is out of
everyone's hands, including ours.
